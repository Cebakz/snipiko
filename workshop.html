Rectangle Workshop

Try a move, see what it leaves behind, and compare it with another choice.

These three boards were made by Cebakz Games for practice. They have no timer, score, clue refill or saved progress. Each choice starts from the same board.

Read coordinates as column then row. A1–C2 spans columns A to C and rows 1 to 2. Choose an option and check the move, or open the solutions to read every result. The board descriptions and solutions also work with JavaScript disabled.

One number, even when the area is right

Which six-square rectangle can you place around the 6?

Choose a rectangle

Compare the solutions
  • A1–C2. Legal. Three columns by two rows makes six. The 6 at B2 is the only clue in the rectangle.
  • B2–D3. Two clues. This rectangle also covers six squares, but it contains both 6 and 2. You can only place one clue at a time.
  • A2–B4. Blocked. This orientation has area six and contains the 6, but B4 is already filled. Rotate or move the rectangle around the clue.

Check the contents as well as the area. Rotating a rectangle preserves its area, but changes which clues and blocks it covers.

See the column as well as the row

Both options are legal. Which opens the most space immediately?

Choose a rectangle

Compare the solutions
  • A5–B5. Legal, but it clears no lines. Both squares stay filled and the 3 is still available for a later move.
  • D3–F3. Legal and clears row 3 and column D together. The two lines share D3, so they clear eleven distinct squares, not twelve. The clue 2 remains.

Before placing a rectangle to finish a row, check whether any of its cells also complete a column. The two-line move clears eleven squares and leaves only the clue 2 on the board. The other move fills two squares without clearing a line.

Give the next rectangle somewhere to fit

Which placement keeps A4–C5 empty as a possible 3 × 2 space for a later 6?

Choose a rectangle

Compare the solutions
  • C3–D4. Legal as a 2 × 2, but it fills C4 and breaks the specific 3 × 2 space we wanted to keep. Other fits could still exist elsewhere.
  • A3–D3. Legal as a 4 × 1. All six squares of A4–C5 stay empty. This gives you one possible shape to use if a 6 later appears in a suitable spot.

Useful room has a shape. In a real game NEXT shows values and the game chooses where clues land. Keeping one space open gives you an option, without guaranteeing the next move.

Check the clue, check the area, then ask what the board will look like afterward. In a real game, look at NEXT again after each move.
